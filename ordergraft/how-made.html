How the pictures and the film were made

Back to the Ordergraft product page.

Rain's file was opened in the full Blender binary under a virtual display (Xvfb); every click and key press is a real X event sent with xdotool, and the screen was recorded with ffmpeg. Ordergraft was installed from the zip a buyer gets. We joined Rain's head, eyes, eyelashes, eyebrows, gums with teeth and tongue into one mesh, applied the Mirror modifiers her parts use, and made the copy by applying her Smile.L, Smile.R, EyebrowsDown.L, EyebrowsDown.R and mouth_open keys at 1.0, 1.0, 0.6, 0.6 and 0.35 to the head part, then renumbering it: the parts joined back in the reverse order and the vertices, edges and faces shuffled by a script of ours, as a round trip through another tool can leave them. The all-triangle pair is the same two meshes triangulated, the source at rest and the copy after its expression, with a different rule for the diagonals. The original index of every vertex is kept apart from the add-on, so each run could be compared with where every vertex belongs; the key errors on the product page come from that. Blender's software renderer draws about one frame a second, so the key slider's moves in the GIFs are stepped by script, one finished frame each; every click, the panels and the Edit Mode view are real recordings. The pointer is drawn into the GIFs from the logged positions, because the screen grab has none. Nothing else was added to the captures except the two labels on the before and after picture and the crops: the first GIF sets the torn face and the Sidebar side by side from the same frames, the panel pictures are the Sidebar, and a result box taller than the window was captured after scrolling the Sidebar with the wheel. The pin GIF uses a copy of her face with her left cornea turned 25 degrees about its own axis; the product frames a listed part no closer than 0.35 m, so the view is zoomed on the eye by a script, Shift is held long enough for Blender to see it, and the waits are sped up; every click and key is real.

The film: the same scene and the same zip, 1920 x 1080. Every click is a real X event; those stretches were recorded at 60 frames a second, and the pointer and a ring at each click are drawn from the logged positions. The slider sweeps were not recorded live: a timer inside Blender set the key's value, drew the whole window, and one screenshot was taken per frame, 60 a second, while the view turned 9 degrees (the tear) or 12 degrees (the clean key). The waits while Blender checks or copies, and a press that missed and was repeated, are cut; Check Pair took about 6 s and the all-triangle Copy Order about 7 s while the screen was being recorded. The captions in the band above the picture, the close-ups of the panel and the closing card are the only things added. A close-up is one window for each caption, its edges between two rows of text, drifting a few pixels sideways, cut from a still of the same panel state that Blender drew at four times the interface size (a 3840 x 4320 screen at 192 dpi with interface scale 2.8, the same zip; the add-on's own operators brought Blender to each state, and the words of the four result states are checked against the take's), shown from the frame at which the recording shows that state, with the pointer and the ring drawn over it where the logged pointer was. A close-up is therefore enlarged about 1.4 to 1.9 times, not five, and it has no highlight of the button under the pointer. The inset in the whole-window shot is cut from the same kind of still. The whole-window shots leave out the Outliner and the Properties editor; the camera moves only at the end, onto the two selected patches, after the Sidebar was closed with the N key.
