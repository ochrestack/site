How we tested Linkrounds

The numbers on the product page and in the quick start, with the times and what we did by hand. Back to the product page.

Where and when

The numbers and pictures on the product page and the quick start were measured with Linkrounds 1.2.7 and Linkrounds Pro 1.2.4 on WordPress 7.1.4 with PHP 8.3.6, on November 18, 2026 (times are London time, which is UTC in November). Both sites are local, on PHP's built-in server with the SQLite Database Integration plugin; screenshots are from Chromium under a virtual display. The film was recorded on November 17 and 18 on the same two sites with Linkrounds 1.2.6 and Pro 1.2.3. Version 1.2.7 changed how the screen lays out its text, where its notices sit and how the address field wraps on a phone, not what a scan finds, so the film shows the earlier screen. Pro installed first was shown on a fresh WordPress 7.1.4 site. The automated tests and the Upload Plugin install check also ran on WordPress 6.5.14, and on both WordPress versions with PHP 7.4.33 (the oldest PHP the plugins support).

"The Hook Weekly", a 300-post archive

A PHP and WordPress link digest we generated for this test: 300 weekly issues from 2014 to 2019 (297 still published), linking to real php.net, developer.wordpress.org, wordpress.org and GitHub pages, some of which are gone. That is 708 different addresses in 2,702 places (links to codex.wordpress.org, a host our test machine cannot reach, were left out). Nobody else visited the site.

  • Scan now took 3 min 7 s with the screen open (one run, 187 s; it depends on how fast the linked sites answer). The picture of the first scan on the product page is from a repeat of the same scan on a fresh copy of the archive, at 4:33 am: the same 708 addresses and 2,702 places, 71 addresses failed once, and 3 min 8 s.
  • First scan, finished 3:23 am: nothing Broken yet. 71 addresses (251 places) failed once, 18 forwarded somewhere (49 places), 619 answered normally (2,402 places), and no site blocked the check.
  • We left Tools > Linkrounds open. The second checks, due from 4:20 am, ran by themselves as soon as the screen was opened again at 4:24 am (our test machine had been restarted in between) and the last one finished at 4:25 am. All 71 failed again and became Broken (251 places). At 4:26 am we requested all 71 Broken addresses ourselves (curl, a browser's User-Agent, redirects followed): 71 of 71 returned 404. So 0 of the 71 Broken addresses were working.
  • Six of the forwards were wordpress.org plugin pages that now land on wordpress.org's plugin search, and the reason says so (below).
  • On the Broken view we ticked rows 4 and 12 of 20 and chose Unlink. We compared all 297 posts before and after: exactly 2 posts changed, and in each only the link's own opening and closing tags were removed.

What a scan costs your server

  • The scan runs in small background batches through WordPress's scheduled tasks (WP-Cron), with a live count and Pause and Resume. If the host kills a batch, the scan picks itself up again.
  • Links to your own posts, pages and uploads are checked against your own database and files, with no request to your site.
  • Each address is requested once per scan, however many posts use it. At most six requests run at once, and at most two go to the same site.
  • A batch starts no new request after 10 seconds and ends within about 25. While it waits for answers it barely uses the processor: on our 300-post archive, a 10-second batch used 1.6 to 2.2 seconds of processor time.
  • Addresses in private or local networks (10.x, 192.168.x, 127.0.0.1) are never requested, except your own site's address. On a local or staging install your site itself is on such an address, so links to your own site that Linkrounds cannot match to a post, page or upload (an archive, a feed, /wp-admin/) are requested from it.

Automated tests

The test suite is 174 tests, plus a separate suite of 21 attack tests written against the first release. Both pass on WordPress 7.1.4 and 6.5.14, each with PHP 8.3.6 and PHP 7.4.33 (the oldest PHP the plugins support). The Upload Plugin check (Pro uploaded first, then the free plugin, through the real Upload Plugin form) passes on the same four, and Plugin Check 2.2.0 reports no errors on the free zip. In Chromium we also checked the results screen’s Enter key, error and result lines, phone layout and Export CSV on both sites, every control as an Administrator, an Editor, an Author and a Subscriber, and installing Pro before the free plugin on a fresh site. Not tested: other WordPress or PHP versions, and multisite in a live install.
