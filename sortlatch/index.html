Why it is needed. Confluence puts every new page at the bottom of the page tree, wherever its title belongs. On our test site, a page created under 40 customer pages in A to Z order landed at position 41 of 41. The request to keep child pages sorted, CONFCLOUD-65454, has over 600 votes and has been Under Consideration since July 2024.

Good to know: Confluence already sorts a parent's children A to Z once, from Space settings > Manage content > Reorder. If a space gets new pages rarely, pressing that button now and then is enough.

Sortlatch is for page trees that keep changing: customers, products, glossary terms, procedures, release notes. It keeps the order without anyone pressing a button, and adds Z to A, newest or oldest first, pins, every level below a page, undo and a space overview. If Confluence ships permanent sorting, Sortlatch is worth only those extras, and this page will say so.

What it does

  • Keeps the order. Choose Keep sorted once on a page. From then on, a child page that is created, renamed, moved in or copied in is sorted into its place within a few minutes, and when someone drags the child pages out of order, the order comes back. On our test site each change was in place 11 to 17 seconds later. A daily check puts back anything that was missed.
  • Numbers in natural order. Title A→Z puts Release 2 before Release 10, ignores upper and lower case, and puts accented letters next to their base letter, so Émile Laurent Associés sits next to Emerald Freight. Title Z→A, Newest first and Oldest first (by creation date) are there too.
  • Pins. Pages that always come first or last, such as an Overview at the top and an Archive at the bottom.
  • One level or all levels below. Sort the child pages of one page, or every level of the page tree under it. Up to 1,000 child pages under one page, sorted in one run with progress on the space overview.
  • A preview before anything moves. It lists each level in its new order, marks every page that will move, and gives the count. Then Sort once, or Keep sorted to save the choice as a rule and sort now.
  • Pages it cannot see. If page restrictions keep Sortlatch out of some child pages, the preview says how many, and the rest are sorted around them only after you confirm. Pages you are not allowed to view are counted, never listed.
  • Undo. Undo last sort puts back what the latest run moved. Restore original order puts back the order from before the rule first sorted, then removes the rule. Pausing or removing a rule never moves a page.
  • A space overview. Space settings > Sortlatch lists every rule in the space with its order, depth, pins, status, last run and pages moved, and why a level was skipped. Sort now, Pause, Resume, Undo, Restore and Remove are on each row. Confluence admins also get one list of every rule on the site.
  • Only the position changes. Moves create no new page version, page content is never edited, and pages never change parent.
  • Only people who can edit a page can sort it. Everyone else is told why.

Documentation

The step-by-step guide is the Sortlatch quick start.

Install

  1. In Confluence Cloud, open Apps > Explore more apps, search for Sortlatch for Confluence (once the listing is live) and choose Get app. A Confluence admin installs it for the whole site and approves the permissions below.
  2. There are no settings. Sortlatch appears in every page's ••• menu as Sort child pages, in Space settings as a Sortlatch tab, and in Confluence admin settings as Sortlatch.
  3. The first time each person opens Sort child pages or the Sortlatch tab, Confluence asks them to allow Sortlatch access. Sortlatch uses that access only to check that the person may edit the page, and to count the child pages the person can see.

Questions

How fast does a new page move?

Within a few minutes. Confluence tells apps about page changes up to 3 minutes after they happen. On our test site it took 11 to 17 seconds (median 11). A daily check puts back anything that was missed.

Does it sort folders?

No. Folders, whiteboards, databases and smart links are not sorted, and neither are pages inside folders. If any of them is among a page's child pages, that level is skipped, and the preview and the space overview say why.

Does it sort the top level of a space?

No. For the pages directly under the space's home page, use Space settings > Manage content > Reorder. A rule with "All levels below" on the home page still sorts every level beneath the top level.

How many pages can it handle?

Up to 1,000 child pages under one page, sorted in one run with progress on the space overview. On our test site, 1,000 shuffled glossary terms took 936 to 938 moves and between 448 and 466 seconds, over five runs. A rule with "All levels below" covers up to 5,000 pages in total. A page with more than 1,000 child pages is skipped, with the reason.

Does sorting create page versions or change content?

No. Only the position among siblings changes. On our test site a moved page stayed at version 1, page content is never edited, and pages never change parent.

What about pages I'm not allowed to see, or that Sortlatch can't see?

The preview never lists pages you can't view; it only counts them. If page restrictions keep Sortlatch out of some child pages, the preview says how many, and the pages it can see are sorted among themselves only after you confirm. The hidden pages stay where they are. When you open the space overview, it counts them directly under each rule's page. Background sorting can't see them, so a page restricted after a rule was saved shows up only when someone opens the overview or the dialog.

Can I undo?

Yes. Undo last sort puts back what the latest run moved and pauses the rule; choose Resume to keep sorting. Restore original order puts back the order from before the rule first sorted, then removes the rule; pages added since stay next to the page they follow now.

What happens when two rules overlap?

One rule per level. A page with its own rule keeps it, and an "All levels below" rule higher up leaves that page's child pages to it.

What happens to a page restored from the trash?

Confluence decides where a restored page goes. On our test site, a page restored through Confluence's REST API came back at the top level of the space, which Sortlatch does not sort. Once a restored page is under a page with a rule, Sortlatch sorts it in.

How are numbers with decimals sorted?

Like version numbers: "Fee 1.5" comes before "Fee 1.25", as v1.5 comes before v1.25. Digits typed in full-width form are not read as numbers.

What if Atlassian ships permanent sorting?

Then Confluence does the main job itself, and Sortlatch is worth only its extras: Z to A, dates, pins, every level below, undo and the overview. This page will say so on the day.

Known limits

  • A few minutes, not instantly. Confluence delivers page events to apps up to 3 minutes after a change.
  • Folders, whiteboards, databases and smart links are not sorted, and a level that has one among its child pages is skipped, with the reason. Pages inside folders are not sorted.
  • The top level of a space is not sorted.
  • Up to 1,000 child pages under one page, and up to 5,000 pages in one "All levels below" rule.
  • Pages Sortlatch cannot see stay where they are. A page restricted after a rule was saved is reported only when someone opens the overview or the dialog.
  • Telling Sortlatch's moves from people's. Sortlatch ignores only changes made by its own account, so a person's drag is always put right. If Confluence ever sends a change without an account, a drag within about 10 seconds of Sortlatch moving that same page can wait for the daily check.
  • Pins are picked from the first level, up to 50.
  • Pages only: blog posts are not sorted. Confluence Cloud only; no Data Center or Server. English only.

How we tested

One test suite installs Sortlatch on a real Confluence Cloud site, creates a space with realistic page trees through Confluence's API, and drives the app through its own backend. Its last runs, on 6 November 2026, passed:

  • Customers, 40 company pages in random order plus one page restricted from the app: the preview counted the hidden page and refused to keep sorting until confirmed; the run moved exactly the pages the preview marked, with no failures; Confluence then listed the 40 pages in natural A to Z order, and the restricted page had not moved.
  • A new page landed at position 41 of 41 before Sortlatch acted, and was in its place 11 seconds later. Renamed, moved-in and copied-in pages, a hand reorder and a page between pins followed (see the table above).
  • Undo last sort put back the 2 pages of the latest run and paused the rule. Restore original order put the 40 pages back in their order from before the first sort and removed the rule.
  • Title Z→A, Newest first, Oldest first, pins at both ends, and All levels below over 4 levels, each read back from Confluence.
  • Glossary, 1,000 shuffled terms: Term 1 to Term 1000 in order after one run, with no failures and progress on the space overview throughout. Sortlatch's own moves set off no further sorting.
  • The folder parent was skipped with its reason, the space home page was treated as the top level, and the site-wide list was shown to a Confluence admin and refused to anyone else.

An automated suite of 110 tests covers the ordering, the preview, keeping sorted for each kind of change, undo and restore, the stated limits, Confluence's documented reasons for refusing a move, and the three screens rendered through Atlassian's own UI Kit code.

Privacy

Nothing leaves Atlassian. Sortlatch runs on Atlassian Forge, calls only your own site's Confluence API, and has no permission to reach any other server. No analytics, no tracking. Its app storage holds page IDs, page titles, positions (the order before a sort, for undo and restore) and the rules you save. It reads no page content and stores none. Uninstalling the app removes its storage. See also the privacy policy.
