Consentwell in detail

What the check does while it runs, how it treats a consent plugin it does not know, what Consentwell Pro adds to the summary, and what Consentwell does not do. The short version is on the quick start; back to the Consentwell product page; what was run to test it is on How Consentwell was tested.

What the check does

While it runs. The page opens in the check window as a visitor who is not logged in and has no consent stored. Consentwell clicks Reject, reloads the page, and watches each load while it moves the mouse, presses a key, touches and scrolls to the bottom (at most 40 steps), until 6 seconds after the last input with nothing new loading.

Server-side check only is the second button: no check window and no Reject click. It fetches the page from your server and lists the script and frame tags in the HTML and the cookies the response sets, HttpOnly ones included. A page that forbids being shown in a frame (X-Frame-Options: DENY) gets this check automatically, and the screen says so.

Which pages. A published page of this site. A page that is not published (a draft, a private or a scheduled page) answers 404 to a visitor, so the screen says that the page exists but is not published. An address that is not a page (a 404, a JSON address, WordPress’s login or admin) stops with one sentence and is not stored.

Reading the result

  • A banner in the HTML but not on screen: the result says what hides it (a class such as cmplz-hidden, display: none, zero size, off screen; the element is on a second line) and a Likely cause box gives one paragraph for it.
  • Causes it can name: no wp_footer() in the theme; a page on Complianz’s list of excluded pages; a script error; the consent plugin’s own files answering an error; a style rule that hides the banner; a hiding class that the consent plugin’s script has not removed; a page-cache copy that differs; Global Privacy Control with the consent plugin set to respect it (hidden by design, nothing is broken).
  • The lists: each request has a type, its full address and a service name where it is well known, beside the cookies the page can read. The consent plugin’s own cookies are counted, not listed. A request that starts later, or only after a real click, is not listed.

Another consent plugin

Complianz and CookieYes are recognised by name. For another banner, Consentwell finds it when it is visible in the check window, has buttons labelled like a choice (Accept, Reject) and mentions cookies or consent. Consentwell first watches the page, and until it asks, the window ignores the mouse and the status line says “Banner found … wait for the prompt before you click Reject.” Then the screen says “Your turn: click Reject in the check window below.” Click the banner’s Reject, and Consentwell continues by itself (or click I clicked Reject, continue).

A banner in a closed shadow root is not found, and Cookiebot’s dialog was not tested with a Cookiebot account. Pro cannot take a manual Reject: Pro clicks Reject on Complianz and CookieYes banners; on any other banner it lists what loads before any choice only.

Why does the banner show to my visitors but not in the check? The check window is a frame, and some banners do not show inside a frame: Cookie Notice & Compliance 3.1.19 stops its own script when a page is in one, and WPConsent 1.2.1 did not appear either (the plugins that were run are under Works with).

Consentwell reports such a banner as in the page HTML but not shown in the check window, with a likely cause, lists what loads before any choice and does not click Reject. Open the page in a private window to see what visitors see. Pro counts these pages on their own summary line. A banner that a script builds only for a top-level window is found by neither check; the result then says “No consent banner found” and suggests the same private window.

Consentwell Pro in detail

  • Pro reads the sitemap that robots.txt declares (such as an SEO plugin’s), or WordPress’s own wp-sitemap.xml.
  • Start over begins a new run.
  • The summary shows only the counters that carry a number as rows; one line names the cases with no page. The table of every page puts each page’s likely cause under its address, and its addresses and cookie names fold out.
  • For pages where Pro cannot click Reject, After Reject reads “not checked”, a notice says how many pages Reject was clicked on, and the CSV has a “Reject clicked” column and the before-choice addresses. Pro clicks Reject on Complianz and CookieYes banners; on any other banner it lists what loads before any choice only.
  • Run it again after you fix something: Since the previous run lists what is new or gone (pages, addresses after Reject, cookies, banners that stopped showing).

Updating. Neither plugin checks for updates by itself. For a new version, download the new zip (Pro: from your order email or order page) and use Plugins > Add Plugin > Upload Plugin, Install Now and Replace current with uploaded.

Deactivate or delete

Deactivating stops the check screens; nothing on your site was changed by a check. Deleting the free plugin also removes its stored results (the last 20 checks). WordPress will not let you deactivate the free plugin while Pro is active; deactivate Pro first.

Limits

  • It does not change, fix or block anything on your site, shows no banner of its own and does not edit your consent plugin’s settings. No score, no legal references, no “compliant” verdict.
  • One location, one browser: you see what a visitor from your own location sees in your own browser. Region-based banners may show other visitors something else. Ad blockers and privacy extensions in your browser can block requests or hide banners; use a profile without extensions.
  • Not listed: a request that starts after the watching ended or only after a real click, one inside a frame (the frame itself is listed), files another domain’s stylesheet loads (the stylesheet is listed), and requests the browser cancelled.
  • Consentwell moves the mouse and scrolls for you. A banner that records a choice on scroll would record one; in the tests, the Complianz and CookieYes banners were still on screen after the scrolling.
  • Your own cookies for the site are set aside during a check and put back with their expiry, path and SameSite. A browser without the Cookie Store API (older Firefox and Safari, or a plain-http admin) cannot read expiry dates: there only cookies named like a consent record are set aside, and they come back as session cookies.
  • Not checked: Accept and per-category choices, Google Consent Mode signals, pages behind a login. No schedules or e-mails; checks run when you click. No network-wide runs on multisite.

Privacy

Neither plugin contacts any server: no account, no licence check, no telemetry, no analytics. Results are stored in your site’s own database (the last 20 checks; for Pro, the current and the previous run). Cookie values are never stored or copied, only cookie names. During a check your site’s own pages are opened in the check window and fetched once by your own server; they load the third-party content they normally load for any visitor, which is what the check measures.
