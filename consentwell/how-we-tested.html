Everything ran on Linux against version 1.0.5 of both plugins. Windows, macOS, MySQL servers other than MariaDB 10.11, Cookiebot banners and browsers other than Chromium were not tested. The images and the demo site are described under how the images were made.

How the images were made

Every image on both pages is a capture of Consentwell 1.0.5 in a real Chromium 153 window under a virtual display, on a local WordPress 7.1.4 (PHP’s built-in server, SQLite) with Complianz Free 7.5.7 set up through its own wizard with the default answers. The demo bakery site has a Contact Form 7 form with reCAPTCHA (Google’s published test keys), a YouTube embed, a Google Map and a theme that loads Google Fonts. The browser, the site and the plugins are real; only the replies of Google’s hosts came from a local stand-in server (the recording machine has no route to them), so every address in the images is one the site’s own pages requested, not a measurement of Google. Online, reCAPTCHA’s api.js also loads a file from gstatic.com. The “Our breads” page uses a template, written for the demo, that does not call wp_footer(). Consentwell Pro is installed only for the Pro images and for the last scene of the film. The “other banner” image replaces Complianz with GDPR Cookie Compliance 5.3.1 from WordPress.org, its Reject button switched on in its own settings, and nothing else changed. The Cookie Notice & Compliance image does the same with Cookie Notice & Compliance 3.1.19 (Reject button on), in a window whose user agent is an ordinary visitor’s, because that plugin hides its banner from what it takes for a crawler. The CSV picture is the file the first Pro run downloaded, shown as a table by a script that changes no cell; it is not a screenshot of a spreadsheet program. The first picture on a phone is two element captures of one result, taken at 320 px wide (the three banner facts and the first After Reject address), joined with a thin gap; what lies between them is left out. The three pictures under it on the product page have a phone version too: the screen captured at 320 px wide with no scrollbar (the status line and the check window with the banner; the result and the likely cause for a page with no banner), and the same CSV shown with three of its four columns.

How the film was recorded

The film on the product page is 59 seconds (59.2 s, recorded on 1.0.5), captions only, no sound. A real Chromium 153 on the demo bakery site (WordPress 7.1.4, Complianz Free 7.5.7 with its wizard defaults, Consentwell and Pro 1.0.5): Tools > Consentwell, the contact page, a page whose template never calls wp_footer(), then Pro over the nine pages of the sitemap. It is recorded at 60 fps with the real pointer and real clicks, 1080p with a 720p copy. The waits are sped up and labelled: 5× for one check, 50× for the Pro run. In the wide cut the interface is drawn at 2.7 times its usual size, and the picture only moves up and down (never sideways or into the screen), so no word is cut at the left or right edge of the frame.

Phones get a second cut, recorded again in a browser window as narrow as a phone (400 px wide, so WordPress shows its own mobile admin), with the interface shown as large as the captions. The browser, the site and the plugins are real; only Google’s replies come from a local stand-in server (see how the images were made).
